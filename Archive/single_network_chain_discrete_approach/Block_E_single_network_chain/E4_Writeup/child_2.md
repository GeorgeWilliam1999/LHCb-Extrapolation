The second method page: what is minimised, how a round of training works, and the five checks that had to pass before a single job was submitted.
# Protocol 4: the loss
The loss is the shared label-free residual, used unchanged from the previous study (`_shared/model.py`, function `physics_loss`):
$$
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{n\,(q+1)\,4} \sum_{\text{states}} \sum_{j=1}^{q+1} \sum_{d=1}^{4} \left( \frac{\hat S_{j,d} - S_d}{\sigma_d} \right)^{2},
$$
the mean over states, over the $`q+1`$ outputs and over the four dynamic components of the squared reconstruction mismatch, where $`\hat S_j`$ is the reconstruction of protocol 2, $`S`$ is the state the network was given, and $`\sigma_d`$ is the divisor described next.
**The divisor is the pooled spread.** $`\sigma_d`$ is the standard deviation of component $`d`$ over the round-1 training states: one number per component, the same for every track and every plane, fixed for the whole run. It makes the four components dimensionless and comparable, and it is the one choice the study after this one changes. The consequence, measured and reported in the results, is that a residual counts for as much as it is large relative to the population, so the objective is dominated by the states with the largest residuals, which are the soft, hard-bending tracks; and it says nothing about where along the crossing the error was made, although an error made early costs far more at the end than one made late.
The stage planes are passed per track, $`z_j = z_{\rm s} + c_j \Delta z`$, because every track's step starts wherever the chain has reached. $`q/p`$ never appears among the four components: it is an input, conserved by construction.
# Protocol 5: training
1. **Rounds.** Round 1 trains on the reference track states at the $`N`$ start planes, because an untrained network's own trajectories are nearly straight lines and would teach it nothing. From round 2 onward, every training track is carried from its real start state at $`z_0`$ through all $`N`$ steps by the network as it currently stands, and the states it predicts on the start planes become the training states. The network therefore learns on the distribution it will actually meet when it is chained.
2. **States per round.** Each round draws 32,000 (track, plane) pairs at random, the same number on every plane, or all of them when there are fewer, which is the case at 2 steps. The states are frozen inputs within a round, and no gradient flows back through earlier steps.
3. **The optimiser.** Full-batch L-BFGS, a quasi-Newton method that builds an approximate inverse Hessian from the last few gradient differences, here with a history of 120, a strong-Wolfe line search and 200 iterations per **restart**. A restart is one run of the optimiser from the current weights with a fresh, empty history.
4. **Rescaling.** The loss is divided by its own value at the start of every round, and again whenever it has fallen tenfold since that constant was set, each time with a fresh optimiser so that the curvature history stays consistent with the objective. A constant factor cannot move the minimum; what it does is keep L-BFGS clear of its fixed absolute tolerances, which is what had quietly ended the previous study's training early.
5. **Round length.** A round ends after 25 restarts, or earlier if two consecutive restarts each gain under 1 per cent. George settled the 25 on 17 September 2026, after the rescaling turned out to remove the stall the plan had relied on to end round 1.
6. **Bookkeeping.** Every restart logs its iteration and function-evaluation counts and an early-stop flag, so that a restart cut short by the optimiser can no longer pass for convergence, and writes a checkpoint, so an evicted farm job resumes where it stopped.
7. **Resources.** One core per run. Four gigabytes is enough except at 16 stages, where 64 and 256 steps exceeded it and the job maker now asks for eight.
# Protocol 6: the gates before the farm
Five checks had to pass before a single job was submitted. All five did, on 16 September 2026.
1. **Model.** At 2 and 256 steps with 2 and 16 stages: a zeroed last layer reproduces the straight line exactly, difference 0; the differentiable field integrals and scales agree with an independent numpy implementation to $`6.4 \times 10^{-16}`$ relative; one batch mixing every start plane equals the per-plane outputs to $`5.7 \times 10^{-14}`$; loss and gradient are finite.
2. **Loss.** On 40 states per case, the exactly solved collocation states, from the earlier study's root-finder with a worst residual of $`5.3 \times 10^{-13}`$ and no failed solves, give a loss of $`4 \times 10^{-35}`$ to $`1.4 \times 10^{-31}`$, against $`5 \times 10^{-6}`$ to 0.11 for the straight line: a ratio of $`10^{-29}`$ or less. This is the check that the residual, with the start plane as a per-track input, really does have the scheme's solution at its minimum.
3. **Output scales.** On 20,000 (track, plane) pairs per step count, the median of the true one-step deviation divided by its scale is 0.89 to 1.01 in $`x`$ and $`t_x`$, with the 99th percentile at most 1.5, and 0.50 to 0.67 in $`y`$ and $`t_y`$, with the 99th percentile 1.0 on the short steps and 3.9 to 5.0 at 2 steps. Quantities of order one are what the last layer can produce. The figure for this gate is `E1_Network_grid/figures/y_scale_check.png`; it is not shown here because its axis titles name the earlier study by its block letter.
4. **Trainer smoke run.** Two steps, two stages, 300 training tracks: stop and resume works and a finished run exits at once; all 150 restarts ran their full 200 iterations with no early stops; the loss fell from 0.19 to $`2.8 \times 10^{-8}`$ through five rescalings and was still gaining 2 per cent per restart at the cap. A second smoke run at 64 steps exercised the round refresh: rounds 2 and 3 trained on the network's own predictions and the validation median at $`z_1`$ went 1,589, then 1,399, then 1,099 µm.
5. **Timing.** One restart at 256 steps and 16 stages on 32,000 states took 200 iterations, 205 function evaluations and 226 seconds on one thread, which is 34 µs per state per evaluation.
