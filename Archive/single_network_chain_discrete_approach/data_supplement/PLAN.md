**Title:** "The data in pictures: what happens to a track between the two planes, drawn to scale"

**Todos relation:** ["https://app.notion.com/p/3e35d544b9d9812b804ac9e34409711d",

**Note (abstract):** one paragraph. Seven figures, drawn from the measured data rather than
sketched, that place every quantity in the problem on its true scale: the 5,177.8 mm crossing,
a 1,205 mm bend at 3 GeV, a 1.7 mm median gap between the field-only reference and the
simulated truth, an 81 µm network error, a 0.147 µm exact scheme and a 0.0285 µm integrator
floor. The figures show where each physical effect enters and which description of the
crossing contains it.

## Structure

Same spine as every write-up here: Intro, Aims, Method, Results, Conclusion, Provenance.
This one is figure-led: the Results section is the seven figures, each with a reading.

### Intro
Why a supplement: the numbers in the companion write-ups are correct but span seven decades,
and a reader cannot hold 5 metres and 80 micrometres in mind at once. State in one paragraph
what a reader will be able to answer after looking: where the detector effects enter, what the
reference reproduces and what it cannot, and what the network is trained on. Name the three
objects once (simulated truth, field-only reference, network) and point at the companion page
for their definitions rather than restating them.

### Aims
Three: put every quantity on one honest scale; show which physical effect enters at which
point in z; separate what is a defect from what is a floor.

### Method (short, this is not a new measurement)
- Every figure is generated by `data_supplement/make_figures.py` from the stored crossing set,
  the field map and the results CSVs; nothing is drawn by hand and nothing is recomputed for
  the page. Say which file each figure draws on, from the README's table.
- The conventions paragraph from the README: equal scale on both axes where possible, the two
  exceptions named (figure 7's stated vertical exaggeration of about 120 times, figure 4's
  logarithmic axis), slopes are dimensionless with "mrad" in older figures meaning a slope
  difference times 1,000, and the metric beside every number (radial or max).
- One paragraph on the case-study tracks: figures 2, 3 and 7 each show one real track chosen to
  sit near the population median, and the medians over all 1,452 test tracks are drawn beside
  them as dotted rings in figure 2. Give the momenta from `figure_facts.json`.

### Results — one subsection per figure, in order
For each: the image, a caption saying what is plotted and on which tracks, then two to four
sentences of reading. Do not pad. The readings, which you should check against
`figure_facts.json` and the CSVs rather than copying from here:
1. **The crossing to scale.** 5,177.8 mm; the bend is 1,205 mm at 3 GeV and falls as 1/p;
   the field peaks at 1.05 T near z = 4,699 mm and is 0.21 T at the start, so the work is done
   in the middle. The curvature in the figure is the real curvature.
2. **The zoom cascade.** Four panels at the far end. The straight line is 342 mm away for this
   track; at ±8 mm the truth separates; at ±400 µm the network separates while the truth is
   off the panel; at ±120 µm the network's own error sits inside the median ring. The order in
   which things become visible is the point.
3. **Where each effect enters.** Five numbered places along z, and the ledger of what each
   description contains. This is the figure that answers the supervisors' question directly.
4. **The error budget.** Seven decades on one axis. Read out the ordering: straight line,
   reference-to-truth, the part of it that is the stale start momentum, network-to-reference,
   the exact scheme, the integrator's floor. Note that above 25 GeV the stale momentum and the
   network error are comparable, and below 10 GeV the gap is dominated by scattering.
5. **The charge split.** The centre flips sign with the charge and the width does not; as a
   fraction of the bend the two charges agree, which is what makes it a momentum deficit of
   16 to 18 MeV rather than a field error.
6. **The scattering floor.** The measured width against momentum with the air-only curve; the
   implied material, 6.7 to 8.1 % of a radiation length over 5 to 200 GeV. Say plainly that
   this is a floor for any deterministic method, not a defect.
7. **One step.** 80.9 mm, two stage states, a departure from the straight line of about 177 µm,
   and the network's own single-step error of 0.43 µm in the inset. This is what the label-free
   loss is written about, and it is why the endpoint error is accumulation rather than a bad step.

### Conclusion
Three sentences of substance, no slogans: the reference is exact for its own equation and the
figures show the equation's scope; one part of the gap is a defect that will be fixed (the start
momentum) and one is a floor that will not (scattering); the network sits well inside both, so
what separates the programme from the truth is now the reference, not the network. Then the
open decisions, pointing at the companion page rather than restating them.
